# Local park and woodland revision

Historical prototype notes. The replacement and verified resource figures are recorded in [3d-rendering-optimization-2026-10-04.md](3d-rendering-optimization-2026-10-04.md). The card canopies described below were replaced with closed leaves, branches and trunks.

The texture budget prototype now applies green landscaping to the whole visible neighbourhood, including the building ring, rather than only the selected apartment site. Enabled with `sceneBudget=texture` on the local preview at port 4195. This revision has not been deployed.

- Unclassified ground and building lots use a green base; registered roads, parking, water and industrial surfaces retain their own surfaces. Paved aprons remain around footprints.
- Both the near paint's edge fade and the native renderer's ground beyond that paint now continue into green terrain. The old edge fade was another source of large cement-coloured slopes.
- Woodland paint mixes green with small soil patches. Forest canopies are dense clusters with four photographic atlas patterns, merged into one indexed mesh per near/far landscaping batch. Each canopy uses six triangles and does not allocate an individual trunk/material/draw call.
- Public green space between neighbouring buildings receives trees, shrubs and flower beds. Footprint and roadway masks prevent planting over buildings or roads. Known water is excluded from planting.
- Nearby park trees have spatially grouped crowns at 3 subdivisions, 8-sided trunks and four main branches. More distant park trees use 2 subdivisions. This restores volume without the original thousands of small leaf cards per tree.
- Twelve existing flower species retain their individual atlas regions; more flower cards are kept in the near band. Grass tufts use a single merged mesh with no alpha texture or shadow caster.
- Balloon route base changed from highest roof +18 m to +38 m; the whole flight and boarding viewpoint move up by 20 m.

## Courtyard, road grading and denser forest follow-up

Courtyard landscaping now preserves registered road, parking, industrial and water parcels when painting the selected site's lawn. Entire bed perimeters receive occupancy checks. Soft soil patches and soil-backed flower borders are limited to open ground within the cadastral site; flowers follow the existing decorative boundary walk. Dedicated surveyed pedestrian paths are not present in the current API, so these flower borders are decorative, not claims of measured garden layouts. Initial plants are filtered against the finished paving/soil/lawn raster, and the late school-border pass retains the courtyard borders. The real browser canvas test finds 577 soil pixels in its courtyard samples, 270 flower-border positions and an unchanged grey parking sample.

A shared road-height bug was reproduced: `continuousRoadGrade` constrained every height in the road bounding rectangle, including nearby mountains outside the road mask. Its upper envelope raised a flat road by 46.04 m in a separated-hill fixture. `corridorRoadGrade` now computes paced minimum/maximum envelopes only through connected road cells, including winding/diagonal roads, then extends that centreline grade across the carriageway and eases it back to the unmodified surrounding DEM. It does not infer surveyed road elevations. The same returned terrain supplies buildings, ground, sidewalks and traffic. The separated hill now leaves the flat road at 0 m; previous cliff, bridge-approach and rendered-ground clearance checks still pass. Desian visual captures before/after show the former huge road-side walls removed.

Near hills without an 임야 parcel previously received no grouped canopy fallback; far hills required height above +35 m relative to the selected complex. Both now use the same local DEM relief classification, independent of that arbitrary relative-height threshold. Existing paving, footprint and water masks still apply. A browser fixture verifies both unregistered near hills and far hills below +35 m receive grouped canopies.

Forest spacing is 5.5 m with wider overlapping crowns. Nearby crowns are merged into larger spatial clumps, split into elevation bands so steep slopes remain distinct. Each clump uses six triangles; arrays allocate once, use 16-bit indices where possible, and avoid temporary vertex vectors. Beyond 140 m, canopy width and spatial merging grow smoothly to 640 m (up to 1.45 times crown radius and 15 m merge cells), increasing distant canopy coverage while limiting geometry. One material and mesh per landscaping batch are retained.

The vertex palette now mixes green (target 56%), gold (18%), orange (12%), red (10%) and bronze (4%) using stable world-coordinate hashing. Four-quadrant tests verify balanced proportions. Park/street trees recolour crowns only: bark and flowers retain their original colours. Forest clumps use the same palette, with no additional texture, material or draw calls.

Lamp placement previously checked only buildings/site bounds and pole spacing, allowing a sidewalk pole to occupy another road or a filled junction. It now checks the union of carriageways plus a safety margin and shares the exact junction hull definitions with asphalt. Survey road segments are stitched before ground/furniture placement so widths agree with traffic and road rendering. Crossing, offset-junction and curved self-overlap fixtures exclude all lamp centres from carriageways.

Current automated regression suite: 37 tests pass. Browser canvas/parcel-worker checks pass, and the production frontend build succeeds. Latest runtime evidence is under `tmp/*ground*.json` and `tmp/gaepo-lamp-check.json`; these local captures include source geometry clearance, lamp-base overlap and forest cluster/palette counts. Loading within 3 seconds and the original intermittent persistent black-facade issue remain unproven. Nothing has been deployed.
